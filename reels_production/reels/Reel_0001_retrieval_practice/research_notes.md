# Reel 0001 — Retrieval Practice Research Notes

## Verified Source Notes

| Source | Evidence type | Verified finding | Boundary for the reel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soderstrom, Kerr & Bjork (2016), *The Critical Importance of Retrieval—and Spacing—for Learning* | Primary experimental research | In one experiment, repeated testing enhanced learning after information could be recalled while restudying did not. In a second experiment that controlled spacing differences within participants, both repeated testing and repeated studying improved learning. | Retrieval and spacing can interact. The reel must not present rereading as useless or imply that recall alone explains every learning benefit. |
| Agarwal, Nunes & Blunt (2021), *Retrieval Practice Consistently Benefits Student Learning* | Systematic review of applied school and classroom research | The review screened nearly 2,000 abstracts, coded 50 experiments, and reported 49 effect sizes with total n = 5,374. It reported that 57% of experiments showed medium or large benefits, while only 6% of experiments were conducted in non-WEIRD countries. | The reel should say retrieval practice is supported across several settings, not that it works identically for every learner or context. The limited non-WEIRD representation is a relevant generalizability caveat. |

## Provisional Claim

> After learning something, trying to recall it from memory can support later retention in many studied contexts. It is not a magic replacement for understanding, feedback, sleep, or sufficient study time; spacing also matters.
