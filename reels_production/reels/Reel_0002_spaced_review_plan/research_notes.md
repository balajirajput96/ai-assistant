# Reel 0002 — Research Notes: Spaced Review Plan

## Claim boundary

This reel will present **spaced review as a learning strategy supported by cognitive and educational-psychology research**. It will not claim that one fixed schedule is medically or universally optimal. The proposed intervals are a practical starter experiment, not a clinical prescription or a guarantee.

## Sources reviewed

| Source | Evidence status | Safe use in the reel |
|---|---|---|
| Carpenter, Pan & Butler, *Nature Reviews Psychology* (2022), “The science of effective learning with spacing and retrieval practice” | Peer-reviewed review article; article page and abstract directly reviewed. | Supports describing spacing and retrieval practice as research-backed strategies used across learning domains and applied educational settings. |
| Cepeda et al., *Psychological Bulletin* (2006), “Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks” | Peer-reviewed quantitative synthesis; publisher-hosted academic record and abstract directly reviewed. It reports 839 assessments from 317 experiments in 184 articles. | Supports saying that the gap between reviews and the time until the desired final recall jointly matter; do not claim a universal best gap. |
| Latimier, Peyre & Ramus, *Educational Psychology Review* (2021), “A Meta-Analytic Review of the Benefit of Spacing out Retrieval Practice Episodes on Retention” | Peer-reviewed meta-analysis; article abstract directly reviewed. It reports an advantage for spaced over massed retrieval practice and no significant universal advantage for expanding over uniform intervals. | Supports pairing spaced sessions with active self-testing, and explicitly rejecting any one-size-fits-all “magic timetable.” |

## Verified framing

The Nature review states that it discusses research findings on **spacing and retrieval practice**, how they can enhance learning across domains and the lifespan, and why effective strategies may be underused because they can be counterintuitive. Cepeda and colleagues' quantitative synthesis reports that spacing and the retention interval jointly influence final-test retention. Latimier and colleagues' meta-analysis found a benefit of spaced over massed retrieval practice, but did not support a universal belief that intervals must progressively expand.

This supports a practical message contrasting repeated short review sessions with a single cram session, while avoiding any claim that a particular interval works best for every learner, subject, or test date.

## Pending verification before scripting

The reel will show a conservative **two-week personal experiment**: recall the material from memory after roughly 1, 3, 7, and 14 days, then adapt the calendar to the real test date. It is a reminder template rather than an evidence-derived universal optimum. Each session must involve recall before re-reading.
