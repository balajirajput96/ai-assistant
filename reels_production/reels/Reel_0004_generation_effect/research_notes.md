# Reel 0004 — Research Notes: Attempt Before Answer

## Claim boundary

This reel concerns **pretesting / errorful generation**: making a genuine attempt to answer a meaningful question *before* studying the correct answer. It will not claim that guessing without feedback is sufficient, that failure is always useful, or that this replaces initial instruction.

## Directly reviewed sources

| Source | Evidence status | Safe use in the reel |
|---|---|---|
| Pan & Carpenter (2023). “Prequestioning and Pretesting Effects: a Review of Empirical Research, Theoretical Perspectives, and Implications for Educational Practice.” *Educational Psychology Review*. | Peer-reviewed open-access review; article page and extracted text directly reviewed. | Supports the definition that pretesting or prequestioning is attempting practice questions prior to learning. The review says this can often benefit learning when learners can subsequently study correct answers, while also noting that results were not positive in all early studies and that design matters. |
| Pan & Sana (2021). “Pretesting Versus Posttesting: Comparing the Pedagogical Benefits of Errorful Generation and Retrieval Practice.” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied*, 27, 237–257. | Peer-reviewed experiment; article first page and abstract directly reviewed from author-hosted PDF. | Supports saying that, across five experiments in that paper, pretesting and posttesting both exceeded a no-test control on later memory tests, and pretesting produced higher overall scores in that set of studies. This is study-specific, not a universal guarantee. |
| St. Hilaire, Chan & Ahn (2024). “Guessing as a learning intervention: A meta-analytic review of the prequestion effect.” *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 31, 411–441. | Peer-reviewed preregistered meta-analysis; article extract directly reviewed. | Supports a key scope limit: prequestions showed a moderate benefit for the initially tested material (g = 0.54, 97 comparisons), but virtually no overall general benefit for new, untested material (g = 0.04, 91 comparisons). |

## Verified framing

The key sequence is **attempt → reveal/correct answer → compare → explain**. The 2023 review states that attempting questions before learning can be beneficial when there is an opportunity to study correct answers afterward. The 2021 experiments do not support a recommendation to leave errors uncorrected; the reel must explicitly require immediate or prompt checking of the explanation.

The 2024 preregistered meta-analysis adds a decisive implementation limit: do not present an attempt as a way to learn *everything* in a lesson. Use it to focus attention on the exact question that is then studied and corrected. The reel will therefore advise one meaningful question at a time, followed by the correct answer and a short explanation.

## Practical implementation for the reel

For a small, meaningful fact or concept, hide the answer, spend about ten seconds making a best attempt, then reveal the answer and explain the mismatch in one sentence. The reel will label this as a study experiment, not a diagnostic exam, and will advise learners to stop using it for emotionally distressing or high-stakes material without appropriate teaching or feedback.
