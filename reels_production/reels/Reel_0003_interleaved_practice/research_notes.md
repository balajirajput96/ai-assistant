# Reel 0003 — Research Notes: Mixed Practice Versus Blocked Practice

## Claim boundary

This reel will distinguish **interleaving** (mixing related problem or example types) from **blocking** (practising one type repeatedly before moving to another). It will not say that mixing is always superior at every learning stage, for every subject, or without initial understanding. The practical recommendation will be to add interleaving after basic examples are understood.

## Directly reviewed source

| Source | Evidence status | Safe use in the reel |
|---|---|---|
| Rohrer, D. (2012). “Interleaving Helps Students Distinguish among Similar Concepts.” *Educational Psychology Review*, 24, 355–367. | Peer-reviewed review article; article page and abstract directly reviewed. | Supports saying that, in several experiments comparing interleaving and blocking, interleaving produced better final-test scores; the article also explicitly states that the evidence is limited and calls for ecologically valid studies. |
| Firth, Rivers & Boyle (2021). “A systematic review of interleaving as a concept learning strategy.” *Review of Education*, 9, 642–684. | Peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis; full article extract directly reviewed. | Supports defining interleaving as mixing examples from different related categories and saying it was most useful when differences between items were subtle. It also supports a limitation: the literature was dominated by laboratory studies of university undergraduates. |
| Samani & Pan (2021). “Interleaved practice enhances memory and problem-solving ability in undergraduate physics.” *npj Science of Learning*, 6, 32. | Peer-reviewed, preregistered classroom study; full article extract directly reviewed. | Supports saying that mixed practice can feel harder during homework and may be judged as less effective, yet in this specific undergraduate physics course students later performed better on novel surprise-test problems. |

## Verified framing

The directly reviewed review and systematic review define interleaving as changing to a different, related category or problem type rather than repeating the same type in a block. The systematic review included 26 eligible studies, with a meta-analysis using 32 datasets from 17 studies. It found interleaving most useful when items are subtly different and readily confusable, while noting that much of the literature used laboratory samples.

Samani and Pan tested ordinary homework ordering over eight weeks in two university introductory-physics lecture sections. Students found interleaved assignments more difficult and believed they learned less, yet the interleaved condition did better on later surprise criterial tests with novel, more challenging problems. This supports the reel's central distinction: **temporary ease while practising is not the same as later ability to choose and apply the right method.**

Therefore, Reel 0003 may contrast a long block of one familiar-looking problem type with a **small mixed set of related types**, but it must frame this as a practical study strategy rather than a universal rule. The recommendation will be to first learn the basic method, then mix closely related types and identify which method each needs.

## Pending verification

Evidence threshold met: three peer-reviewed sources have been directly reviewed. The reel will not claim that unrelated subjects should be shuffled randomly, that interleaving replaces initial instruction, or that it guarantees a specific score increase.
